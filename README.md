# Abstract

Leptin acts directly and indirectly on diverse neuronal populations to regulate feeding and numerous other physiologic functions. Yet how a single circulating signal organizes diverse neuronal populations into coherent feeding circuits remains poorly understood. To further elucidate its effect on gene expression in its direct neural targets and downstream mediators, we combined time-resolved single-nucleus transcriptomics and chromatin accessibility profiling to map leptin-induced molecular state transitions across individual neurons in two key hypothalamic nuclei: the arcuate nucleus (ARC), which contains the majority of leptin receptor (LepR) neurons, and the paraventricular hypothalamic nucleus (PVH), an established downstream target of ARC LepR neurons. We found that leptin treatment of leptin-deficient ob/ob mice does not alter neuronal identity, but instead induces coordinated, cell-type-specific transcriptional and regulatory state changes that propagate hierarchically from the ARC to downstream PVH neurons. Integrating RNA velocity, cis-regulatory analysis, and cell-cell communication inference revealed that leptin modulates hypothalamic circuit architecture by selectively strengthening specific interactions while pruning broadly distributed, low-specificity signaling. Although leptin broadly activated STAT3 signaling across LepR neuronal subtypes, integrated multi-omic analyses revealed that shared STAT3 activation engages distinct downstream regulatory programs in different neuronal populations, often with opposing physiological functions. Functional analyses identified the homeobox transcription factor OTP, a leptin-responsive regulator selectively induced in AGRP neurons, as a critical mediator of leptin-mediated inhibition of hunger-promoting AGRP neuronal activity and feeding. In parallel, unbiased analysis of PVH neuronal state transitions shows that NOVA1, an RNA-binding protein primarily expressed in inhibitory neurons, is regulated by leptin and alterations of NOVA1 expression bidirectionally control food intake and body weight. Together, these findings define a multi-layered regulatory framework through which leptin regulates hypothalamic feeding circuits to control energy balance.
